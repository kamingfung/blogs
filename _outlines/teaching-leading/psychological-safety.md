**Promise:** Explain what psychological safety is and is not, and use three leader habits that make it cheaper for people to report a mistake or disagree.

**Trap:** Psychological safety means being nice and lowering standards; it means people can take the risk of speaking up, and it works best alongside high standards.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/belonging-and-engagement.

## Outline

1. **From belonging to speaking up**: belonging asks "am I one of us?"; safety asks "what happens if I say I was wrong?"; describe the idea, then name it as psychological safety `[verify]`.
2. **Safety and standards are two axes**: high safety with low standards is comfortable; low safety with high standards is anxious; the target is both high `[verify]`.
3. **What the leader controls**: frame the work as uncertain, admit your own errors first, and answer bad news with a question instead of blame.
4. **Signals that it is missing**: problems surface late, meetings agree and corridors disagree, the same few people speak.
5. **What this means for you**: in the next week, report one of your own mistakes in public, and thank the next person who brings you bad news before you fix anything.

## Key takeaways

- Psychological safety is about the cost of speaking up, not about comfort or praise `[verify]`.
- Safety without standards produces comfort, not learning; keep both high.
- The leader's response to the first piece of bad news sets the price for every later one.
- Late surprises are a better signal of low safety than a survey score.
