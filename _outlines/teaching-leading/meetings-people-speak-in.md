**Promise:** Use a few structures (silent writing first, a round, a written channel) so that more than the usual two or three people contribute in a work meeting.

**Trap:** An open floor gives everyone a chance to speak; in practice it gives the floor to the fastest and most senior voices, and others stay quiet.

**Assumes:** Nothing beyond the active-learning point in teaching-leading/belonging-and-engagement that unstructured discussion favors confident voices.

## Outline

1. **Who talks in an open floor**: the same few people speak first, and the first idea anchors the rest; seniority adds weight to a voice.
2. **Think before talk**: two minutes of silent writing before discussion, the meeting version of think-pair-share; everyone arrives with a view.
3. **Rounds and order**: go around the room, and ask the most junior person first when the topic invites deference.
4. **More than one channel**: a pre-read with comments, a chat during calls, and a written follow-up let people who think slowly or speak a second language contribute.
5. **What this means for you**: in your next meeting, start with two minutes of silent writing, then take one point from each person before open discussion; note who spoke.

## Key takeaways

- Silent writing before discussion gives every person a view to share.
- Asking junior people first reduces anchoring on the senior view `[verify]`.
- A written channel next to the spoken one brings in people who do not speak first.
- Track who spoke for two or three meetings; the pattern is usually clear without a survey.
