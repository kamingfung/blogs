title: Presenting data to a decision
level: intermediate
---

**Promise:** Present an analysis to a decision-maker by starting from the decision, showing only the charts that bear on it, and stating the uncertainty in terms of what it means for the choice.

**Trap:** Showing all of the analysis proves rigor; in fact a decision-maker shown every chart has to find the relevant one alone, and trusts the analysis less when they cannot.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/answer-first.

## Outline

1. **Start from the decision, not the data**: name the choice on the table and the options; every chart you show must move someone toward or away from one option.
2. **One chart, one point**: give each chart a headline that states its finding, label it directly, and remove what does not support that finding.
3. **Uncertainty as a decision quantity**: translate error bars into "how likely is it that option A is better" or "what would have to be true for B to win"; a range alone does not tell a manager what to do.
4. **The backup deck**: keep method, robustness checks and the charts you cut ready for questions; showing them only when asked is not hiding them.
5. **What this means for you**: before the next presentation, write the decision at the top of your notes and strike every chart that does not change it.

## Key takeaways

- If you cannot say which option a chart supports, the chart belongs in the backup deck.
- Labeling lines and bars directly on the chart, instead of in a legend, lets the audience read the chart while you talk [verify].
- Stating uncertainty as its effect on the choice gets more useful questions than showing an interval with no interpretation.
- A presentation that names the decision in its first minute gets discussion about the decision, not about the method.
