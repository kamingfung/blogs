**Promise:** Run a two-minute check during a session and use the answers to change what you teach next.

**Trap:** Formative assessment is a quiz that does not count toward the grade; a check is formative only if its answers change what the teacher or learner does next.

**Assumes:** Nothing required; it builds on the feedback section of teaching-leading/meeting-students-where-they-are.

## Outline

1. **Defined by use, not by form**: the same question is formative when it steers the next step, and summative when it only records a result; describe the idea, then name both terms `[verify]`.
2. **Quick checks**: a one-minute paper, a "muddiest point", an exit question, a show of answer cards; describe each as a prompt and what you learn from it `[verify]`.
3. **Ask a question that separates**: a good check has wrong answers that reveal a specific misconception, not just right or wrong.
4. **Closing the loop**: start the next session with what the check showed and what you changed; learners keep answering when they see their answers used.
5. **What this means for you**: end your next session with one question on a card, sort the answers in five minutes, and open the next session with what you found.

## Key takeaways

- A check is formative only if its result changes the next step for the teacher or the learner.
- A short check at the end of a session finds misunderstandings before they reach the graded work.
- A question whose wrong answers map to known misconceptions tells you what to reteach.
- Learners keep giving honest answers when they see those answers change the teaching.
