**Promise:** Restructure any update, email or talk so the conclusion comes in the first sentence and the reasons follow in the order the reader will question them.

**Trap:** You must build up to a conclusion so the audience trusts it; in fact a busy reader who meets the conclusion last often never reaches it, and a reader who meets it first can judge each reason against it.

**Assumes:** Nothing.

## Outline

1. **The lab-report habit**: scientists are trained to write background, method, results, conclusion; that order serves a referee checking the method, not a manager making a decision.
2. **Lead with the answer**: state the conclusion or the request, then the few reasons that support it, then the detail behind each reason; this is the idea behind the pyramid principle [verify].
3. **Backward design, again**: answer first is backward design for one message: decide what the reader must know or do, then choose only the content that gets them there.
4. **When answer first goes wrong**: an answer the reader will resist, a question with no answer yet, and how to state "I don't know yet, here is what would tell us" as the answer.
5. **What this means for you**: rewrite one recent message: move the last sentence to the top, cut what no longer supports it, and check that the first line alone is enough.

## Key takeaways

- If a reader stops after your first sentence, they should still know your conclusion or your request.
- Ordering the supporting reasons by the question the reader will ask next keeps the message short, because each reason answers one question.
- When there is no answer yet, the answer-first sentence is what you will decide, by when, and what evidence would settle it.
- A message that cannot be opened with its conclusion usually means the writer has not decided the conclusion yet.
