**Promise:** Run a calibration session where several raters score the same sample work against one rubric, find where they disagree, and fix the rubric's wording or the raters' reading.

**Trap:** A calibration meeting is where managers rank people against each other to fit a quota; in fact calibration means making several judges apply the same standard, which is what graders of the same exam do.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/rubrics-for-hiring.

## Outline

1. **Two graders, one essay, two grades**: a shared rubric does not guarantee a shared reading; raters drift apart in strictness and in which criteria they weight.
2. **How teachers calibrate**: grade the same anchor samples alone, compare, discuss the gaps, rewrite the anchors; repeat at the start of each grading round.
3. **Calibration is not ranking**: contrast agreeing on a standard with forcing results into a fixed distribution; the second answers a budget question, not a quality one.
4. **Running it at work**: the same steps for interview panels and review cycles: shared samples, independent scores, discussion of the largest gaps only, a written record of each decision.
5. **What this means for you**: a one-hour session plan, and the signal that tells you the rubric itself, not the raters, needs fixing.

## Key takeaways

- Raters who use the same rubric still disagree until they have scored and discussed shared examples together [verify].
- Discussing only the items with the largest score gaps uses the meeting time where agreement actually changes.
- When most disagreements come from one criterion, rewrite that criterion's anchors instead of retraining the raters.
- A forced distribution of ratings decides how many people get each score; it does not make any rater's judgment more accurate [verify].

- **Show:** a small grid of three raters by five samples, before and after one calibration round, so the reader sees the spread shrink.
