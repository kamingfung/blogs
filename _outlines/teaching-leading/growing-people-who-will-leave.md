---

**Promise:** Treat a team the way a teacher treats a class that will graduate: plan each person's growth past their current role, talk openly about their next step, and build the team so it survives their departure.

**Trap:** Developing people makes them leave, so a manager should protect the team by keeping them where they are; in fact people who stop growing tend to leave anyway, and the team loses them without the goodwill or the handover [verify].

**Assumes:** Nothing in the series file; reads best after teaching-leading/mentoring.

## Outline

1. **Every student graduates**: a teacher's success is measured by where students go next; a team lead can adopt the same measure and stop treating departures as failures.
2. **Talk about the next job early**: ask what the person wants in two or three years and plan work that builds toward it, even when the answer is a different team or employer.
3. **Build a team that outlasts its members**: documentation, rotation and paired work so that no departure takes the only copy of a skill.
4. **Alumni are part of the team's reach**: people who leave on good terms refer candidates, collaborate and sometimes return [verify].
5. **What this means for you**: one career conversation per person this quarter, and a list of the skills on the team that live in only one head.

## Key takeaways

- Asking each person where they want to be in a few years, and planning work toward it, tells you about a likely departure early instead of on resignation day.
- Lack of growth is a common reason people give for leaving a job [verify]; withholding development does not remove that reason.
- A skill that only one person on the team has is a risk whether or not that person plans to leave.
- People who leave a team that helped them grow are a source of referrals and future collaboration [verify].
