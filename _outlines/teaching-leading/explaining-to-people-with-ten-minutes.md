**Promise:** Prepare a ten-minute explanation for a busy, non-specialist listener by choosing one idea, linking it to what they already know, and deciding in advance what to drop.

**Trap:** With little time you should talk faster and cover everything in compressed form; in fact a listener retains one or two ideas from a short slot, so covering more means they keep less.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/answer-first.

## Outline

1. **Ten minutes is a lesson, not a lecture**: treat the slot like one chunk of a class: one learning outcome, stated before you prepare a single slide.
2. **Start from what they know**: apply the prior-knowledge idea to a listener: find the concept they already hold that your idea attaches to, and the misconception it must displace.
3. **Choose the idea, cut the rest**: list everything true and interesting, then keep only what the one outcome needs; the method stays in the appendix unless they ask.
4. **Check, don't assume**: ask one question that shows whether the idea landed, the spoken version of a quick formative check.
5. **What this means for you**: a preparation sheet: the outcome sentence, the familiar idea it hooks onto, the three things you will say, the list of things you will not say.

## Key takeaways

- Writing down the one thing the listener should be able to repeat afterwards, before preparing anything else, shortens preparation and the talk.
- An analogy to something the listener already uses lands faster than a definition, provided you also say where the analogy breaks.
- Deciding in advance what to cut means that when the meeting runs late you drop the planned material, not the conclusion.
- Asking the listener to say back the main point in their own words shows you whether the explanation worked, which a nod does not.
