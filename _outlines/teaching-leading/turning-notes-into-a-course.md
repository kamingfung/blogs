**Promise:** Turn a pile of notes or blog posts into a course: write the outcomes the notes support, order them by prerequisite, find the gaps, and add a check for each outcome.

**Trap:** A course is the notes put in a sensible order; in fact notes record what the writer found interesting, and a course needs what the learner needs, including the steps the writer now finds too obvious to write down.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/backward-design, teaching-leading/aligning-assessment.

## Outline

1. **Notes are written forward**: they follow the order the writer learned or worked, which is forward design by default; a course needs to be planned backward.
2. **Outcomes first, then sort the notes**: write the outcomes the course should reach, then tag each note with the outcome it serves; notes that serve none are cut or moved to an appendix.
3. **A prerequisite map shows the gaps**: draw which idea depends on which; missing links are the steps an expert skips without noticing [verify].
4. **Add the checks**: for each outcome, one task or question that shows a learner has it; this is the part notes almost never contain.
5. **What this means for you**: the procedure this series itself follows: a syllabus file of modules, levels and prerequisites, outlines for the gaps, and posts written against the outlines.

## Key takeaways

- Tagging each note with the outcome it serves shows at once which notes are surplus and which outcomes have no material.
- Drawing a prerequisite map of your notes finds the steps you skip because you know them too well.
- Notes almost never contain a way for the reader to check their understanding; adding one check per outcome is most of the work of making a course.
- Writing each module's promise and trap before the module itself stops neighboring modules from repeating each other.
