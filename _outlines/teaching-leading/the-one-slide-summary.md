**Promise:** Build a single slide that stands on its own: a headline that states the claim, one piece of evidence that supports it, and the decision or next step.

**Trap:** A one-slide summary means fitting the whole deck onto one page in small type; in fact it means choosing the one claim the deck exists to make and leaving the rest behind.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/answer-first.

## Outline

1. **The slide that travels**: the summary slide gets forwarded, pasted into other decks and read without you; it must work with no speaker.
2. **Headline as a sentence**: replace topic titles ("Q3 results") with a claim ("Costs fell because of X"); the headline is answer first in one line.
3. **One piece of evidence**: choose the single chart, number or table that makes the headline believable, and label it so it needs no narration.
4. **The ask**: end with what the reader should decide or do, and by when; a summary with no ask leaves the reader to guess.
5. **What this means for you**: a test: read only the headlines of your deck in order; if they do not tell the story, the one-slide summary is not written yet.

## Key takeaways

- If a slide's headline is a topic instead of a claim, the reader must work out the point alone, and different readers will work out different points.
- One well-labeled piece of evidence on a summary slide persuades more than several small ones the reader cannot read when the slide is forwarded.
- Reading only the headlines of a deck in order is a quick test of whether the deck has an argument.
- A summary slide that ends with a specific ask and date gets a decision more often than one that ends with "discussion" [verify].
