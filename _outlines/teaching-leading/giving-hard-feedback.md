**Promise:** Tell someone that a piece of their work or behavior is not good enough, in a way that is specific, soon and about the work, and check that they heard it.

**Trap:** Wrapping criticism between two compliments makes it easier to hear; often the listener remembers the praise and misses the point `[verify]`.

**Assumes:** Nothing required; it applies the feedback rules from teaching-leading/meeting-students-where-they-are to adults at work.

## Outline

1. **Why we avoid it**: the giver's discomfort, fear of damage to the relationship, and the false kindness of letting a problem grow.
2. **Behavior, impact, request**: describe what happened, what it caused, and what you ask for next time; describe the idea, then name a common form of it `[verify]`.
3. **About the work, not the person**: the same rule as process praise; "this analysis skipped the check" instead of "you are careless".
4. **Soon, private and short**: the closer to the event, the more useful; one issue per conversation; no audience.
5. **Check that it landed**: ask the person to say what they will change, and agree when you will look at it again.
6. **What this means for you**: pick one piece of feedback you have postponed, write it as behavior, impact and request in three sentences, and deliver it this week.

## Key takeaways

- Name the behavior, its impact and the request; leave out guesses about intent or character.
- Give one hard point per conversation, soon after the event, in private.
- Ask the person to say back what they will change; their words show whether it landed.
- A problem you do not name is a decision to let it continue.
