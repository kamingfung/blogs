**Promise:** Plan a meeting the way you plan a class session: one stated outcome, an agenda timed in short blocks, an activity that makes people work, and a closing check that the outcome was reached.

**Trap:** A meeting is a place to share information; in fact information sharing works better in writing, and a meeting earns its time only when the people in it must think or decide together.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/active-learning.

## Outline

1. **Does this need to be a meeting**: apply the first question of backward design: what should be true when people leave; if nothing needs a group to make it true, send a written update instead.
2. **The agenda as a lesson plan**: each item states its outcome (decide, generate options, agree on a risk) and its time; information items go in a pre-read.
3. **Make people work**: borrow active-learning structures: silent writing before discussion, pairs before the whole group, a quick vote; these get a view from everyone before the loudest view sets the direction.
4. **Close like a class**: restate what was decided, who does what by when, and ask what is still unclear; send it in writing within the day.
5. **What this means for you**: rewrite your next recurring meeting's agenda as outcomes with times, move one status item into writing, and add one silent-writing step.

## Key takeaways

- An agenda item written as an outcome ("decide X") ends with a decision more often than one written as a topic ("X update").
- Two minutes of silent writing before an open discussion gets ideas from more people than starting the discussion directly [verify].
- Moving status updates into a written pre-read frees meeting time for the items that need the group.
- A meeting that ends without a restated list of decisions and owners will be remembered differently by each attendee.
