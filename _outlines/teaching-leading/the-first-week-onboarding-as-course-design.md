**Promise:** Plan a new teammate's first week backward from what they should be able to do by its end, with one small real task and a check of what they already know.

**Trap:** Onboarding is giving access and a reading list; reading does not produce the ability to do the work, and a new person cannot tell which pages matter.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/backward-design, teaching-leading/psychological-safety.

## Outline

1. **Outcomes for a first week**: write two or three things the new person can do by Friday, for example run the main tool or explain who uses the team's output.
2. **Find out what they bring**: a short conversation about past tools and gaps beats a fixed curriculum; the same person may be expert in one area and new in the next.
3. **One small real task**: a task that ships in the first days teaches the workflow end to end and gives an early success.
4. **Who to ask, and permission to ask**: name a buddy, say out loud that questions are expected, and schedule the first check-ins in advance.
5. **What this means for you**: before the next person joins, write their Friday outcomes, pick the first task, and book two short check-ins.

## Key takeaways

- Write what a new person can do by the end of week one, then plan backward from it.
- A first real task that ships teaches more of the workflow than a week of reading.
- Ask what the new person already knows before you choose what to teach.
- Saying that questions are expected, and naming who to ask, removes the most common first-week blocker.
