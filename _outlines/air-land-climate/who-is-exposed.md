**Promise:** Explain why the pollution a person breathes differs from the reading at the nearest monitor, and which groups carry the highest exposure.

**Trap:** Everyone in a city breathes the city's air quality number; exposure depends on where people live, work and cook, and for how long.

**Assumes:** air-land-climate/what-pm2-5-is-and-why-2-5.

## Outline

1. **Concentration versus exposure**: exposure is concentration times time spent in it, so indoor air, commuting and outdoor work all count.
2. **Where in the world**: population-weighted PM2.5 is highest in South Asia, East Asia, and parts of Africa and the Middle East, where dust and combustion overlap with dense populations. [verify]
3. **Indoors and in the kitchen**: households that cook with wood, dung or coal can breathe more particles indoors than any outdoor monitor reports. [verify]
4. **Near roads, near sources, outdoors at work**: people near highways, industry or fields that burn, and people who work outside, face higher doses; in many countries these groups are also poorer.
5. **What this means for you**: field crews breathe more air per hour during hard work and spend the afternoon outdoors, when ozone peaks and smoke can arrive; plan exposure by work hours, not by daily averages.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- More than 90% of the world's population lives where annual PM2.5 exceeds the WHO guideline. [verify]
- Household solid-fuel use exposes over two billion people to high indoor PM2.5. [verify]
- In the US, studies find higher average PM2.5 exposure in lower-income and minority communities. [verify]
- Outdoor workers receive a higher dose than residents at the same concentration because of longer time outdoors and heavier breathing.
