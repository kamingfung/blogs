**Promise:** Convert between the common air-quality units, read a standard as a concentration plus an averaging time plus a compliance rule, and say what an index category like "unhealthy" does and does not claim.

**Trap:** Air below the standard is safe and air above it is dangerous; a standard is a policy line drawn on a risk curve with no clear threshold.

**Assumes:** air-land-climate/what-pm2-5-is-and-why-2-5 and air-land-climate/ozone-good-up-high-bad-nearby.

## Outline

1. **Mass per volume and mixing ratios**: particles are reported in micrograms per cubic meter, gases often in parts per billion; converting needs temperature and pressure, which is why the same ppb gives different micrograms on a mountain.
2. **A standard has three parts**: a level, an averaging time (one hour, 8 hours, 24 hours, a year) and a form (for example the annual fourth-highest daily value averaged over three years), and changing any one changes how strict it is.
3. **Guidelines and standards differ**: the WHO guideline is a health-based target, while national standards weigh feasibility and cost; the gap between them can be several-fold. [verify]
4. **How an index turns concentration into a color**: air quality indexes map each pollutant through piecewise-linear breakpoints and report the worst one; countries use different breakpoints, so "unhealthy" in one index is not the same air in another.
5. **What this means for you**: before using air-quality data as a model feature or a work rule, check the unit, the averaging time and which index produced a category; a daily index value can hide a bad afternoon hour for field crews.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- At 25 °C and 1 atmosphere, 1 ppb of ozone is about 2.0 micrograms per cubic meter. [verify]
- The 2021 WHO annual PM2.5 guideline is 5 micrograms per cubic meter, below most national standards. [verify]
- An air quality index reports the worst single pollutant, so its category changes when the dominant pollutant changes even if total risk does not.
- Two standards with the same numeric level can differ in strictness because of their averaging time and form.
