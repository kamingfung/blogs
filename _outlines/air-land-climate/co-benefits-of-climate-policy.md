**Promise:** Explain where climate action improves air quality, where cleaner air warms the climate, and why the health gains arrive sooner and closer to home than the climate gains.

**Trap:** Air policy and climate policy always pull the same way; removing sulfate and nitrate particles cleans the air but takes away some of their cooling.

**Assumes:** air-land-climate/what-the-clean-air-act-did and air-land-climate/black-carbon.

## Outline

1. **Same sources, two problems**: burning fossil fuels emits carbon dioxide alongside sulfur dioxide, NOx and particles, so cutting combustion cuts both.
2. **Local and soon versus global and later**: health benefits of cleaner air appear within years in the place that acts, while the climate benefit of less carbon dioxide is global and slow. [verify]
3. **The unmasking penalty**: sulfate and nitrate particles cool; removing them adds warming in the near term, which partly offsets the carbon dioxide benefit. [verify]
4. **Wins on both sides**: cutting methane lowers ozone and warming, and cutting black-carbon-rich sources helps both; cutting farm ammonia lowers PM2.5 but slightly reduces nitrate cooling. [verify]
5. **What this means for you**: farm measures that cut nitrous oxide or ammonia can be counted for climate and for air quality, but check the sign of each effect rather than assuming every cut helps both.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Monetized health co-benefits of carbon dioxide cuts often match or exceed the cost of the cuts in the near term. [verify]
- Removing anthropogenic sulfate aerosol produces near-term warming. [verify]
- Methane reductions lower surface ozone in addition to slowing warming. [verify]
- Cutting agricultural ammonia lowers PM2.5 but slightly reduces aerosol cooling through less ammonium nitrate. [verify]
