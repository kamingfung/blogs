**Promise:** Explain what wildfire smoke is made of, how it travels thousands of kilometers, and what it does to people, ozone and crops downwind.

**Trap:** Smoke far from a fire is diluted and harmless; smoke can arrive days later at unhealthy levels, and it keeps changing chemically as it travels.

**Assumes:** air-land-climate/primary-and-secondary-aerosols and air-land-climate/what-pm2-5-is-and-why-2-5.

## Outline

1. **What is in smoke**: mostly organic particles with some black carbon, plus carbon monoxide, NOx and many organic gases; most of the particle mass is PM2.5.
2. **Plume rise and long-range transport**: hot fires loft smoke above the boundary layer, where winds carry it across continents; it reaches the ground when it mixes down.
3. **Smoke ages**: organic material evaporates and recondenses, and smoke mixed with city NOx can raise ozone downwind. [verify]
4. **Health and crops**: smoke PM2.5 may be more harmful per microgram than other PM2.5; for crops, smoke scatters and dims sunlight, which can raise or lower photosynthesis, and can taint grapes. [verify]
5. **What this means for you**: smoke events raise afternoon exposure for field crews, and the arrival time comes from transport forecasts, not local fire maps; for yield models, smoke days are a distinct weather condition, not just cloudy days.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Organic aerosol makes up most of wildfire smoke particle mass. [verify]
- Wildfire smoke now accounts for a growing share of PM2.5 in the western US as other sources decline. [verify]
- Smoke plumes can raise ozone downwind, especially when they mix with urban NOx. [verify]
- Thin smoke can increase canopy photosynthesis by adding diffuse light, while thick smoke reduces it. [verify]
