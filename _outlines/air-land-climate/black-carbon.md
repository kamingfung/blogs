**Promise:** Explain how black carbon warms the climate in ways other aerosols do not, and why cutting a black-carbon source is not always a net climate win.

**Trap:** Black carbon warms, so every cut in soot cools the planet; the sources that emit it also emit cooling particles, and the net sign depends on the mix.

**Assumes:** air-land-climate/primary-and-secondary-aerosols.

## Outline

1. **What counts as black carbon**: soot from incomplete combustion that absorbs light at all visible wavelengths and survives high temperatures; "black carbon", "elemental carbon" and "refractory black carbon" are names tied to different measurement methods.
2. **Three ways it warms**: it absorbs sunlight in the air, it darkens snow and ice where it lands, and it changes clouds by heating the layer it sits in.
3. **Short life, local effect**: black carbon rains out within days to weeks, so its warming is patchy and stops soon after emissions stop, unlike carbon dioxide. [verify]
4. **The co-emission problem**: diesel engines emit mostly black carbon, while residential wood and crop burning emit much more organic carbon, which cools; the net effect of a cut depends on the source.
5. **What this means for you**: for health, cutting black-carbon sources helps almost everywhere; for climate, the source matters, so a diesel fleet upgrade and a stove program are not interchangeable offsets.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Black carbon absorbs sunlight across the visible spectrum, while most other aerosol components mainly scatter it.
- Black carbon's atmospheric lifetime is days to about two weeks, so its climate effect responds within weeks to emission changes. [verify]
- Black carbon deposited on snow lowers its albedo and speeds melt, an effect concentrated in the Arctic and high mountains. [verify]
- Sources with a high ratio of organic to black carbon, such as biomass burning, can have a net cooling or near-zero climate effect. [verify]
