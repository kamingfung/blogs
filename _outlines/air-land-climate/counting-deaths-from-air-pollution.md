**Promise:** Explain how a headline number of deaths from air pollution is calculated, what it means, and why published totals differ by a factor of two.

**Trap:** "Millions of deaths from air pollution" counts people whose death certificates name it; it is a modeled number of deaths that would not have occurred, or would have occurred later, at a lower concentration.

**Assumes:** air-land-climate/exposureresponse-curves.

## Outline

1. **The recipe**: deaths equal population times baseline death rate times the attributable fraction, where the fraction comes from the exposure–response curve at the local concentration.
2. **The counterfactual**: every estimate compares with a chosen clean level; moving that level changes the total, so "deaths from" really means "deaths avoided if".
3. **Why totals disagree**: global estimates range from about four to about nine million a year, mostly because of the curve and counterfactual chosen, not the exposure data. [verify]
4. **Deaths, years and timing**: an attributed death may be months or decades early, so years of life lost and life expectancy change are often more honest measures.
5. **What this means for you**: when a study reports deaths avoided by a policy or a farming change, check the curve, the counterfactual and the baseline death rate before comparing it with another study.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Attributable deaths are computed as population times baseline mortality times the attributable fraction from a relative risk curve.
- Global estimates of deaths from ambient PM2.5 differ by roughly a factor of two between major studies. [verify]
- The choice of counterfactual concentration can change a national total as much as the exposure data do.
- Attributable deaths cannot be summed across risk factors, because one death can be attributed to several causes.
