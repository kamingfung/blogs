**Promise:** Say what each of the three main measurement types actually measures, where each one is biased, and which to trust for a given question.

**Trap:** A satellite air-quality map shows the air at the ground; satellites see the whole column of air, and turning that into a surface concentration needs a model.

**Assumes:** air-land-climate/what-pm2-5-is-and-why-2-5 and air-land-climate/units-standards-and-what-unhealthy-means.

## Outline

1. **Reference monitors**: regulatory instruments weigh filters or use approved continuous methods; they are accurate but sparse and sited for compliance, not for farms.
2. **Low-cost sensors**: optical particle counters infer mass from scattered light, so humid air, fog and coarse dust bias them; they need collocation with a reference monitor and correction. [verify]
3. **Satellites**: instruments measure aerosol optical depth or a column of nitrogen dioxide over the overpass time, blocked by clouds; converting to surface concentration uses a model of the vertical profile.
4. **Choosing a source for the question**: compliance needs reference data, local variation needs sensors, regional patterns and remote areas need satellites, and most real products blend all three.
5. **What this means for you**: a sensor on a farm during smoke or fog needs a humidity correction before its PM2.5 is usable as a model feature; a satellite value at one overpass is not a daily mean.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Reference PM2.5 methods measure dried particle mass, while optical sensors measure light scattering from particles at ambient humidity.
- Uncorrected low-cost PM sensors overestimate PM2.5 at high relative humidity. [verify]
- Satellite aerosol optical depth measures the whole atmospheric column and correlates with surface PM2.5 only through the vertical profile and humidity.
- Polar-orbiting satellites sample a location about once a day, at a fixed local time. [verify]
