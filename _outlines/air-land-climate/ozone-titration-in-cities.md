**Promise:** Explain why ozone is often lower in a city center than in its suburbs and countryside, and why cutting traffic can raise ozone at a city monitor.

**Trap:** Where there is more pollution there is more ozone; fresh nitric oxide from traffic destroys ozone locally, so city centers often read lower and the ozone shows up downwind.

**Assumes:** air-land-climate/nox-vocs-and-sunlight and air-land-climate/why-ozone-peaks-in-the-afternoon.

## Outline

1. **Fresh NO eats ozone**: nitric oxide from exhaust reacts with ozone to make nitrogen dioxide within minutes; near busy roads and at night this removes ozone without making any back.
2. **Ozone is moved, not destroyed**: the oxygen atom ends up in nitrogen dioxide, so the sum of ozone and nitrogen dioxide (total oxidant) stays nearly level across a city while ozone alone dips.
3. **The urban dip and the downwind bulge**: maps show low ozone in the core and higher ozone in suburbs and rural areas downwind, where the same precursors have had hours to react.
4. **Natural experiments**: weekends and pandemic lockdowns cut traffic NOx, and urban ozone often rose while nitrogen dioxide fell. [verify]
5. **What this means for you**: a city monitor understates the ozone that farms and orchards downwind receive; use rural sites or total oxidant when estimating crop exposure.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- The reaction of nitric oxide with ozone removes ozone near fresh NOx sources but conserves total oxidant, ozone plus nitrogen dioxide.
- Annual mean ozone is often lower in urban cores than at nearby rural sites. [verify]
- During 2020 lockdowns, many urban sites saw ozone rise as NOx fell. [verify]
- Ozone formed from city emissions often peaks tens of kilometers downwind, where crops and forests are exposed. [verify]
