**Promise:** Weigh the carbon a new forest takes up against its effects on surface brightness, air chemistry, water and land, and say where planting trees cools and where it may not.

**Trap:** More trees always cool the planet; a dark canopy over snowy or dry land can absorb more sunlight than its carbon offsets, and trees emit gases that change ozone and particles.

**Assumes:** air-land-climate/stomata-and-dry-deposition and air-land-climate/leaf-area-index.

## Outline

1. **Carbon in, sunlight in**: a growing forest stores carbon, but it is darker than grass, crops or snow, so it absorbs more sunlight.
2. **Latitude decides the sign**: tropical forests cool through carbon and evaporation; boreal forests can warm by hiding snow; temperate cases are close to neutral. [verify]
3. **Trees change the air**: forests emit isoprene and terpenes that feed ozone where NOx is high and form organic particles that scatter light; they also take up ozone through stomata.
4. **Water, permanence and land**: plantations use water, can burn or die, and compete with farmland, which may push clearing elsewhere.
5. **What this means for you**: a tree-planting offset is only as good as its location, its permanence and what land use it displaced; ask for all four effects, not only the tonnes of carbon.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- The albedo effect of boreal afforestation can outweigh its carbon uptake and produce net warming. [verify]
- Tropical afforestation tends to cool both through carbon uptake and through evaporative cooling. [verify]
- New forests near NOx sources can raise ozone through isoprene while also removing ozone by deposition.
- Afforestation that displaces farming can shift land clearing elsewhere, reducing its net carbon benefit.
