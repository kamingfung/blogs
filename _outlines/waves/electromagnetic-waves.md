**Promise:** Explain light, radio and X-rays as one kind of wave, a traveling electric and magnetic field, and say why they interact with matter so differently.

**Trap:** Readers treat radio, visible light and X-rays as different things; they are the same wave at different frequencies, and their different effects come from how much energy each photon carries.

**Assumes:** waves/the-wave-equation and waves/transverse-and-longitudinal (the series file lists none).

## Outline

1. **A wave with no medium**: changing electric fields make magnetic fields and the reverse, so the pair can travel through empty space.
2. **The speed falls out**: the wave equation from earlier, with constants from electricity and magnetism, gives the speed of light.
3. **Transverse, so it can be polarized**: the fields point across the direction of travel; polarizing sunglasses and screens use this.
4. **One spectrum**: radio to gamma rays on one frequency axis; wavelength sets what the wave can resolve and photon energy sets what it can do to molecules.
5. **Matter picks the frequency**: why glass passes visible light, why X-rays pass through soft tissue, and why leaves reflect near-infrared.
6. **What this means for you**: vegetation indices from satellites and drones work because healthy leaves absorb red light and reflect near-infrared strongly; the index is a contrast between two bands of the same wave.

## Key takeaways

- An electromagnetic wave is a pair of electric and magnetic fields at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel.
- Maxwell's equations predict a wave speed equal to the measured speed of light. [verify]
- All electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed in vacuum and differ only in frequency and wavelength.
- Photon energy is proportional to frequency, which is why X-rays can ionize atoms and radio waves cannot.
- Healthy leaves absorb strongly in red light and reflect strongly in near-infrared, the basis of vegetation indices such as NDVI. [verify]
