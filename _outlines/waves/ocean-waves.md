**Promise:** Explain why long ocean swells outrun short waves, why waves slow and steepen near shore, and why a tsunami crosses an ocean at jet-aircraft speed.

**Trap:** Readers assume all ocean waves travel at the same speed, as sound does; in deep water longer waves travel faster, and in shallow water the depth, not the wave, sets the speed.

**Assumes:** waves/describing-a-wave (the series file lists none).

## Outline

1. **Gravity is the spring**: water lifted into a crest is pulled back by gravity; water parcels move in near-circles and do not travel with the wave.
2. **Dispersion in deep water**: wave speed grows with wavelength, so a storm's mixed waves sort themselves into long, regular swell that arrives first.
3. **Shallow water and the shore**: once depth is small compared with the wavelength, speed depends only on depth; waves slow, bunch up, steepen and break, and bend toward shallower water.
4. **Tsunamis are shallow-water waves**: with wavelengths of hundreds of kilometers, even the deep ocean is shallow for them, so they travel fast and grow sharply near shore.
5. **Describing a messy sea**: a real sea is many waves at once, summarized by a spectrum and by significant wave height.
6. **What this means for you**: wave and coastal records are among the most direct places where spectra of a random signal are used every day, and sea-state statistics are a ready-made exercise in spectral analysis for environmental data.

## Key takeaways

- In deep water, the speed of a surface gravity wave increases with the square root of its wavelength.
- In water much shallower than the wavelength, wave speed equals the square root of gravity times depth.
- Swell from a distant storm arrives with its longest-period waves first.
- In the deep ocean a tsunami travels at several hundred kilometers per hour. [verify]
- Significant wave height is defined from the wave spectrum and is about the mean height of the highest third of waves. [verify]
