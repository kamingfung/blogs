**Promise:** Plan public engagement as a two-way conversation with a specific audience about something they care about, rather than as an announcement of your result.

**Trap:** People doubt science because they lack the facts, so outreach should supply more facts; in fact more information alone rarely changes views, and relevance, trust and dialogue matter more.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/explaining-to-people-with-ten-minutes (a short explanation for a non-expert).

## Outline

1. **The deficit model and why it disappoints**: the assumption that the public is an empty vessel, and what the evidence says happens when you fill it.
2. **Start from the audience's question**: a farmer, a school class and a city council want different things from the same science; find out before preparing.
3. **Saying what is uncertain**: how to state uncertainty honestly without inviting the reading that nothing is known.
4. **Formats that allow a reply**: school visits, public Q&A, community workshops and writing that invites response, compared with one-way releases.
5. **What this means for you**: a plan for one outreach activity, starting from a named audience and their question.

## Key takeaways

- Providing more scientific information alone shifts attitudes on contested topics less than the deficit model predicts. [verify]
- Audiences rate scientists who state uncertainty openly as at least as trustworthy as those who do not. [verify]
- An outreach activity planned from the audience's own question gets more questions back than one planned from the speaker's result.
- Press releases often overstate the findings of the paper they describe. [verify]
