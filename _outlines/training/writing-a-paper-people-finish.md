**Promise:** Restructure a research paper so a reader who stops after the title, abstract and figures still leaves with the finding, and one who reads on finds each section answering the question they now have.

**Trap:** A paper is a record of what you did, in the order you did it; in fact it is an argument ordered by the reader's questions, and the chronology belongs in the methods.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/answer-first (the claim before the support).

## Outline

1. **How papers are actually read**: most readers see the title, the abstract and the figures, and few reach the discussion; design for that drop-off instead of against it.
2. **The paper as one argument**: state the finding in one sentence before drafting; every section either supports it, qualifies it, or is cut to the supplement.
3. **Figures first, text around them**: draft the figure sequence as the storyboard; each results paragraph opens with the claim its figure shows.
4. **Paragraphs a skimmer can follow**: topic sentence carries the point, old information before new, one term per concept from abstract to discussion.
5. **What this means for you**: a revision pass that reads only first sentences and figure captions, and checks that they tell the whole story alone.

## Key takeaways

- A reader who reads only the first sentence of each paragraph and the figure captions should be able to state the paper's finding.
- Results ordered by the question they answer read faster than results ordered by the date the analysis was run.
- Writing the one-sentence finding before the first draft shortens revision, because it gives a test for what to cut.
- Readers spend far more time on titles, abstracts and figures than on the body of a paper. [verify]

## Phase 2

- **Show:** the same results section twice, once in lab-notebook order and once in question order, with only the first sentences visible.
- **Sources to find:** studies of how scientists read papers (reading surveys, eye-tracking or download-versus-read data); journal and style-guide advice on paper structure; writing-research on given-new ordering.
