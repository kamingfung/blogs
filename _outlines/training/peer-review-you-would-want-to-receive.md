**Promise:** Write a referee report that tells the editor whether the conclusions follow from the evidence and tells the authors exactly what would fix each problem.

**Trap:** A referee's job is to find faults and decide acceptance; in fact the editor decides, and the referee advises on soundness and value, with a concrete fix for every objection.

**Assumes:** training/feedback-that-starts-from-strengths (specific feedback about the work, strengths included).

## Outline

1. **What the editor needs from you**: a judgment on whether the question matters and whether the conclusions follow, separate from style and taste.
2. **Before you accept**: scope, conflicts of interest, time you can actually give; declining fast is a service.
3. **The shape of a report**: a short summary in your own words, the main concerns ranked, minor points last; each concern with the evidence that would resolve it.
4. **Tone and limits**: write the review you would sign; say which parts you could not judge; no requests for the paper you would have written instead.
5. **What this means for you**: a report template and a final pass that deletes every objection with no proposed fix.

## Key takeaways

- A summary of the paper in the referee's own words lets the editor and authors check that the referee understood it before reading the objections.
- An objection paired with the analysis or data that would resolve it is more often addressed in revision than one stated alone. [verify]
- Agreement between independent referees on the same manuscript is low. [verify]
- Saying which parts of a paper the referee could not evaluate helps the editor choose additional referees.

## Not in this module

- training/feedback-that-starts-from-strengths: feedback between colleagues on work in progress.
- teaching-leading/code-review-as-feedback: review inside a team, from a lead's side.
- training/writing-a-paper-people-finish: the author's side before submission.

## Phase 2

- **Show:** one weak referee comment rewritten into a ranked concern with a proposed fix.
- **Sources to find:** editorials and guides on refereeing from major journals; studies of inter-reviewer agreement; publishers' and committee guidance on reviewer ethics and conflicts of interest.
