**Promise:** Build a conference or seminar talk around one message, with slides that show rather than document, and rehearse it to time.

**Trap:** A talk is the paper read aloud with slides; in fact a listener cannot reread, so a talk carries one idea well and points to the paper for the rest.

**Assumes:** teaching-leading/explaining-to-people-with-ten-minutes (fitting an explanation to a short slot).

## Outline

1. **Listening is not reading**: a listener cannot skip back, so structure has to be said out loud: where we are, where we are going.
2. **One message, then the cut**: pick the sentence the audience should repeat at the coffee break; every slide that does not serve it moves to backup.
3. **Slides as figures, not documents**: one point per slide, the headline states the claim, the speaker carries the words the slide leaves out.
4. **Rehearsal and questions**: rehearse aloud to a clock, cut to fit the slot with margin, and treat questions as part of the talk, prepared for in advance.
5. **What this means for you**: a checklist for the week before a talk, from message to timed run-through.

## Key takeaways

- If an audience member cannot state your one message after the talk, extra content did not help them.
- A slide headline that states a claim is remembered better than a headline that names a topic. [verify]
- A talk rehearsed aloud to a timer runs closer to its slot than one rehearsed silently. [verify]
- Nervous arousal before speaking is common and does not by itself predict a worse talk; the evidence on what reduces it needs checking before any advice is given. [verify]
