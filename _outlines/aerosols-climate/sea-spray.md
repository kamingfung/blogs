**Promise:** Explain how breaking waves throw salt and organic particles into the air, why wind speed controls how much, and why sea salt dominates aerosol mass over the ocean but not the number of cloud seeds.

**Trap:** Sea spray is the visible spray at the shore; the particles that matter for clouds come from tiny bubbles bursting far out at sea, and most are too small to see.

**Assumes:** cloud-formation/where-ccn-come-from (primary and secondary particle sources).

## Outline

1. **Bubbles, not splashes**: breaking waves trap air; bubbles rise and burst; the thin cap shatters into many small film drops, and the collapsing cavity shoots up a few larger jet drops [verify].
2. **Wind sets the supply**: whitecap cover rises steeply with wind speed, so emissions do too, which makes the stormy Southern Ocean a major source [verify].
3. **What is in a sea spray particle**: salt, and an organic film skimmed from the sea surface, especially where plankton are active; the drops dry to salt particles in the air.
4. **Mass versus number**: sea salt dominates aerosol mass over remote oceans, but much of the cloud-seed number there can come from sulfate made from marine gases [verify]; large salt particles still matter as the head start for rain.
5. **What this means for you**: coastal farms and monitors see sea salt as part of their measured particulate mass, which is natural rather than pollution; salt carried inland on onshore winds settles on leaves and soil near the coast.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Most sea spray particles come from bursting bubbles in whitecaps, not from wind tearing spray off wave crests.
- Sea spray emission rises steeply with wind speed, roughly with the third power or more [verify].
- Sea salt dominates aerosol mass over the remote ocean, while a substantial share of cloud condensation nuclei there comes from secondary sulfate [verify].
- Sea salt particles are very good cloud seeds because salt takes up water readily, so even a few large ones can speed up rain formation.
