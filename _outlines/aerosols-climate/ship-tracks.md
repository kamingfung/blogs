**Promise:** Read a satellite image of ship tracks as a natural experiment: say what it proves about particles brightening clouds, and what it fails to settle about cloud water and climate.

**Trap:** Ship tracks show that pollution makes clouds brighter, case closed; the droplet effect is clear, but the cloud's water can rise or fall in response, and that response decides the climate answer.

**Assumes:** aerosols-climate/aerosol-indirect-effects.

## Outline

1. **Lines in the clouds**: long, bright lines in low marine cloud seen from space, each following a ship's path; the same water, more droplets.
2. **A natural experiment**: the ship adds particles to one narrow strip while the air on either side stays untouched, so the strip and its neighbors act as treated and control.
3. **What the tracks confirm**: more and smaller droplets in the track, and a brighter cloud, as predicted.
4. **What they do not settle**: in some tracks the cloud thickens, in others it thins as drier air mixes in from above; only some ships leave tracks at all [verify]; the fuel-sulfur limit for ships as a large, recent test [verify].
5. **What this means for you**: for the student, ship tracks are a template for using a sharp, known emission change as an experiment; there is no direct farm tie.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Ship tracks show directly that adding particles to marine low cloud increases droplet number and reduces droplet size.
- The cloud's liquid water can increase or decrease in a ship track, depending on the humidity above the cloud and whether it was raining [verify].
- Most ships do not leave a visible track; tracks form mainly in shallow, moist marine boundary layers [verify].
- The global cut in ship fuel sulfur in 2020 offers a large natural experiment on aerosol-cloud effects, and its estimated warming effect is still debated [verify].
