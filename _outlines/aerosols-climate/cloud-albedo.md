**Promise:** Explain what sets the fraction of sunlight a cloud reflects (how much water it holds, how that water is divided into droplets, and how much of the sky it covers) and why adding water brightens a thin cloud far more than a thick one.

**Trap:** A cloud's brightness tracks how much water it holds; the same water split into more, smaller droplets reflects more, and a thick cloud is already so bright that more water barely changes it.

**Assumes:** aerosols-climate/do-clouds-warm-or-cool.

## Outline

1. **Albedo is a fraction**: the share of incoming sunlight sent back up; the dark ocean beneath reflects little, so a cloud over the sea makes the biggest contrast [verify].
2. **Optical thickness**: light scatters off droplets many times; the number of scattering events through the cloud depends on total droplet surface area, which grows with liquid water and shrinks with droplet size.
3. **Albedo saturates**: the curve of albedo against optical thickness rises fast and then flattens, so thin clouds are the most sensitive to any change [verify].
4. **Cloud fraction and the scene**: a broken cloud field reflects less than its cloudy parts; satellites see the average of cloud and gaps; why marine stratocumulus decks dominate the global reflection budget [verify].
5. **What this means for you**: for the student, cloud albedo is the lever that every aerosol-cloud effect pushes on; for forecasting, thin low cloud is where small errors in cloud water give the largest errors in surface sunlight.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- A cloud's optical thickness grows with its liquid water path and falls with its droplet size.
- Cloud albedo rises with optical thickness but saturates, so a given change in water or droplet size moves the albedo of a thin cloud far more than that of a thick one.
- At fixed liquid water, a cloud with more, smaller droplets has a higher albedo.
- Low marine stratocumulus over the dark ocean contributes more to Earth's reflected sunlight than any other cloud type [verify].
