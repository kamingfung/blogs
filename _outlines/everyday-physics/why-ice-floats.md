**Promise:** Explain why solid water is less dense than liquid water when most solids sink in their own melt, and why lakes freeze from the top.

**Trap:** "Ice floats because it has air bubbles trapped in it." Clear, bubble-free ice floats too; hydrogen bonds lock frozen water molecules into an open lattice that takes up more room than the liquid.

**Assumes:** Something floats if it is less dense than the liquid around it. No module required.

## Outline

1. **Most solids sink**: in most substances the solid packs tighter than the liquid, so solid wax or metal sinks in its own melt [verify]; water is the odd one out.
2. **The open lattice**: each water molecule can hold four neighbors by hydrogen bonds at fixed angles; in ice all four are held, which forces a roomy hexagonal pattern about 9% less dense than the liquid [verify].
3. **Liquid water is partly collapsed**: in the liquid, bonds break and re-form all the time, so molecules crowd closer; water is densest near 4 °C [verify].
4. **Lakes freeze from the top**: water near 4 °C sinks and colder water stays on top and freezes, so the ice lid insulates the water below.
5. **What this means for you**: water expands as it freezes, so freezing bursts pipes, heaves soil and ruptures cells in frozen produce, which is why thawed strawberries go soft [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Ice floats because its molecules are held in an open lattice, not because of trapped air.
- Water expands by about 9% when it freezes [verify].
- Liquid water is densest at about 4 °C [verify].
- Lakes freeze from the top down, and the bottom water of a deep lake stays near 4 °C in winter [verify].
