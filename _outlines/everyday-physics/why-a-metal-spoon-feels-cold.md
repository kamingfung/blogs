**Promise:** Explain why a metal spoon and a wooden spoon at the same temperature feel different, and predict which feels hotter on a hot day.

**Trap:** "The metal spoon is colder than the wooden one." Both sit at room temperature; skin senses how fast heat leaves it, and metal draws heat away faster.

**Assumes:** Heat flows from hot to cold. No module required.

## Outline

1. **Same temperature, different feel**: a thermometer reads the same on both spoons; the difference is in the hand, not the spoon.
2. **Skin measures heat flow**: nerve endings respond to the temperature of the skin itself, which falls fast if heat drains away fast.
3. **Why metal drains heat fast**: free electrons carry heat through metals hundreds of times better than wood conducts it [verify]; the contact point settles near the temperature of whichever material moves heat more readily.
4. **The flip side**: in a sauna or on a sunny car, metal above body temperature feels much hotter than wood at the same temperature, for the same reason.
5. **What this means for you**: frozen food thaws faster on an aluminum tray than on a wooden board, because the metal feeds room heat into it faster.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Objects in the same room settle to the same temperature, whatever they are made of.
- Touch senses the rate of heat flow out of the skin, not the temperature of the object.
- Metals conduct heat about a thousand times better than wood [verify].
- Metal hotter than the skin feels hotter than wood at the same temperature.
