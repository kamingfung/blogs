**Promise:** Explain why metals reflect most visible light, and why gold and copper are colored when silver and aluminum are not.

**Trap:** "Metals are shiny because they are smooth and polished." Smoothness only makes a reflection sharp; a metal reflects because its free electrons move with the light's electric field and send the light back, so even crumpled foil reflects most of the light.

**Assumes:** Light is an oscillating electric field. everyday-physics/why-glass-is-transparent (absorption needs a matching energy step).

## Outline

1. **A sea of free electrons**: in a metal, some electrons belong to no single atom and move freely through the solid.
2. **Electrons that answer back**: the light's field shakes these electrons, and their motion makes a field that cancels the light inside and sends it back out; the light gets only a few tens of nanometers in [verify].
3. **Shiny or matte is a separate question**: a smooth surface reflects in one direction, like a mirror, and a rough one scatters in all directions; both reflect most of the light.
4. **Why gold is yellow**: in gold and copper, bound electrons absorb blue and green light, so the reflected light is yellow or reddish [verify]; silver and aluminum reflect all visible colors about equally.
5. **What this means for you**: reflective ground covers in orchards send sunlight back up into the lower canopy, where it can improve fruit color [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Metals reflect visible light because free electrons move with the light's field and re-radiate it.
- Surface smoothness controls whether reflection is mirror-like or diffuse, not how much light is reflected.
- Fresh aluminum reflects about 90% of visible light [verify].
- Gold is yellow because its bound electrons absorb blue light, an effect that depends on relativity [verify].
- Above a certain frequency, in the ultraviolet, the free electrons cannot keep up and many metals become partly transparent [verify].
