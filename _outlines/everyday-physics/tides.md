**Promise:** Explain why most coasts get two high tides a day, and why spring and neap tides follow the Moon's phases.

**Trap:** "The Moon pulls the sea toward it, and the second bulge on the far side is thrown out by the Earth's spin." The far bulge comes from the Moon pulling the Earth's center harder than the far ocean; tides come from the difference in pull across the Earth, not the pull itself.

**Assumes:** everyday-physics/why-orbits-don-t-fall (the Earth falls freely around the Earth-Moon center of mass). Not listed in the series file's `requires`.

## Outline

1. **The pull is not the point**: the Moon pulls the whole Earth; the whole Earth falls toward it, so only the difference between near side, center and far side is left over.
2. **Two bulges from one difference**: the near ocean is pulled more than the center and the center more than the far ocean, so the water stretches both ways along the Earth-Moon line.
3. **The Sun joins in**: the Sun pulls harder than the Moon but its difference across the Earth is less than half as strong [verify]; lined up, they add for spring tides, at right angles they partly cancel for neap tides.
4. **Real coasts are not bulges**: oceans cannot keep up with a bulge sweeping round the planet, so tides slosh around points of no tide in each basin, and coast shape sets the local range and timing.
5. **What this means for you**: in river deltas the tide pushes salt water upstream, and in dry seasons it can reach irrigation intakes for rice and other crops [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Tides come from the difference in the Moon's pull across the Earth, which falls off with the cube of distance [verify].
- The bulge on the far side forms because the Earth's center is pulled toward the Moon more than the far ocean is.
- The Sun's tidal effect is a bit less than half the Moon's [verify].
- Spring tides happen near new and full Moon, neap tides near the quarter Moons.
- Some coasts get one high tide a day, not two, because their basin shape favors that rhythm [verify].
