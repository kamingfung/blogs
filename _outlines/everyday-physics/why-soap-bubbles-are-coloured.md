**Promise:** Explain bubble colors as light reflected from the two faces of a thin film adding or canceling, and read a film's thickness from its color.

**Trap:** "The bubble splits white light into colors like a prism." The film is far too thin to spread colors by bending; colors appear because light reflected from the front and back of the film adds up for some wavelengths and cancels for others.

**Assumes:** Light is a wave, and two waves can add or cancel. everyday-physics/rainbows (color by bending), for contrast.

## Outline

1. **Two reflections**: a soap film reflects a little light from its outer face and a little from its inner face.
2. **Add or cancel**: the second reflection travels farther, by twice the thickness; where that extra trip puts the two waves in step, a color is bright, and out of step, it is missing.
3. **Thickness paints the bubble**: films a few hundred nanometers thick show strong colors [verify]; the film drains and swirls, so the colors move and form bands, thinner at the top.
4. **Black before it pops**: a film much thinner than the wavelength reflects almost nothing, so a black patch appears just before the bubble breaks [verify]; oil on a puddle shows the same colors for the same reason.
5. **What this means for you**: the same effect made deliberately, as antireflection coatings, lets more light through glasses, camera lenses and some greenhouse glass [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Soap-bubble colors come from interference between reflections off the two faces of the film, not from the soap's color.
- The color at a point tells you the film's thickness there.
- A film thinner than about a tenth of a wavelength looks black [verify].
- An antireflection coating is a film about a quarter wavelength thick that makes the two reflections cancel [verify].
