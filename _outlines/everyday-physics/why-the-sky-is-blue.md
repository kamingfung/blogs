**Promise:** Explain why clear sky is blue, sunsets are red and hazy sky is white, from how particles of different sizes scatter light.

**Trap:** "The sky is blue because it reflects the ocean" (or "because dust scatters blue"). The sky is blue over deserts too; the nitrogen and oxygen molecules of the air themselves scatter blue light much more than red.

**Assumes:** White sunlight is a mix of colors with different wavelengths. No module required.

## Outline

1. **Scattering by things much smaller than light**: a molecule shaken by a light wave sends out a little light in all directions, and the shorter the wavelength, the more it sends.
2. **Blue wins by a large factor**: scattering grows with the fourth power of frequency, so blue light is scattered several times more than red [verify].
3. **Why not violet**: sunlight holds less violet than blue, the upper air absorbs some, and our eyes are less sensitive to violet [verify].
4. **Red sunsets, white haze**: at sunset light crosses much more air and loses its blue on the way; droplets and dust are larger than the wavelength and scatter all colors about equally, so haze and clouds look white.
5. **What this means for you**: on hazy or thinly cloudy days more light arrives from the whole sky rather than straight from the Sun, and this diffuse light reaches shaded leaves better [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Air molecules scatter blue light much more than red light because they are far smaller than the wavelength.
- Blue light of 450 nm is scattered about five times more than red light of 700 nm [verify].
- The sky is not violet because of the Sun's spectrum and the eye's color sensitivity, not because violet is not scattered.
- Particles about the size of the wavelength or larger scatter all colors about equally, which is why clouds and haze are white.
