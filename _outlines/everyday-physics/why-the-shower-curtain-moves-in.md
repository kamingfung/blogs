**Promise:** Weigh the candidate causes of the inward-billowing shower curtain and say which survive a simple test.

**Trap:** "Fast air has low pressure, so Bernoulli sucks the curtain in." Bernoulli's rule compares pressure along one flow, not between a moving jet and the still air outside; the spray drags air along and sets up a swirl whose low-pressure core sits inside the shower, and warm-air rise adds to it.

**Assumes:** Pressure pushes on surfaces; warm air rises. No module required.

## Outline

1. **What has to be true**: the curtain moves in only if the air pressure on the inside face is lower than on the outside face.
2. **Candidate one, warm air rising**: hot shower air rises and draws air in at the bottom; but cold showers move the curtain too, so this cannot be the whole story.
3. **Candidate two, Bernoulli**: the rule links speed and pressure along a streamline in steady flow; applying it across the curtain to still bathroom air is the misuse.
4. **Candidate three, the spray drags air**: falling drops pull air down with them (entrainment); that air must be replaced, and a simulation found a swirl with low pressure at its center inside the shower [verify]; how settled this is.
5. **What this means for you**: the same air-dragging by sprays carries fine droplets sideways, one of the causes of spray drift from crop sprayers [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- The curtain moves in because pressure inside the shower drops below the bathroom's pressure.
- Warm-air rise cannot be the only cause, because a cold shower also pulls the curtain in.
- Bernoulli's rule does not apply between two separate air masses that are not on one streamline.
- A computer simulation of a shower found a horizontal swirl with a low-pressure core driven by the spray [verify].
