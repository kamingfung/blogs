**Promise:** Explain why visible light passes through glass but not through stone or wood, in terms of which photons the electrons can absorb and what can scatter the rest.

**Trap:** "Light slips through the gaps between the atoms in glass." Glass is as tightly packed as many opaque solids; it is clear because its electrons cannot absorb visible photons and its smooth, uniform structure has nothing to scatter them.

**Assumes:** Light comes in packets (photons) whose energy rises as the color goes from red to blue. everyday-physics/why-broken-solids-cannot-re-bond helps for the picture of atoms held in a solid.

## Outline

1. **To be absorbed, a photon must fit a jump**: an electron takes in light only if the photon's energy matches a step up to a free energy level.
2. **Glass has a gap too wide for visible light**: the electrons in silica need ultraviolet photons to jump, so red to violet pass through untouched, and ultraviolet is stopped [verify].
3. **Nothing to scatter from**: glass has no grains or crystal boundaries; ground glass, sand and snow are opaque only because their many surfaces scatter light every which way.
4. **Where glass gets its color**: traces of iron tint the edge of a window green, and added metals color bottles and stained glass [verify].
5. **What this means for you**: greenhouse glass passes the visible light plants use and stops most ultraviolet and most outgoing infrared [verify]; much of a greenhouse's warmth comes from trapped air rather than trapped infrared [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- A material is transparent to a color when its electrons have no energy step that matches that color's photons and nothing inside scatters it.
- Window glass absorbs most ultraviolet below about 300 to 350 nm [verify].
- Clear glass turns white when ground to powder because the many surfaces scatter light, not because the material changes.
- The green tint seen through the edge of a window pane comes mainly from iron impurities [verify].
