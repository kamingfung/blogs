**Promise:** Explain why a rainbow is a circle at a fixed angle from the point opposite the Sun, why red is outside, and why a second bow is reversed.

**Trap:** "A rainbow is an arc standing at a place in the sky, and each raindrop holds a whole rainbow." A rainbow is a set of directions, not a place: each drop sends you one color, different drops make each band, and every observer sees their own bow.

**Assumes:** Light bends when it passes between air and water, by a slightly different amount for each color. No module required.

## Outline

1. **One drop, one path**: sunlight enters a drop, bends, bounces once off the back and bends again on the way out.
2. **Light piles up at one angle**: most exit angles are spread thin, but many rays leave near about 42° from the incoming direction, so that angle is bright [verify].
3. **Each color has its own angle**: red leaves at a slightly larger angle than violet, so red is on the outside; you see each color from drops on a circle around the point opposite the Sun.
4. **The second bow and the dark band**: two bounces inside give a fainter bow near 51° with colors reversed [verify], and the sky between the bows is darker because no once- or twice-bounced light reaches you from there.
5. **What this means for you**: you see a rainbow only with the Sun behind you and rain in front, so a morning rainbow is in the west, where weather in the mid-latitudes often comes from [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- A rainbow is always centered on the point directly opposite the Sun.
- The main bow sits about 42° from that point, which is why you cannot see one when the Sun is higher than about 42° [verify].
- Red is on the outside of the main bow and on the inside of the secondary bow.
- The sky between the two bows is darker than elsewhere [verify].
- Two people standing apart see rainbows made by different drops.
