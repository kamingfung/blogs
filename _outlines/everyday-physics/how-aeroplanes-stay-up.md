**Promise:** Explain wing lift as the wing turning air downward, and say what the pressure difference and the angle of the wing have to do with it.

**Trap:** "Air over the curved top must meet the air under the wing at the back edge, so it goes faster and the pressure drops." There is no such meeting rule; air over the top arrives well before the air underneath, and a wing lifts by deflecting air downward, which flat and upside-down wings also do.

**Assumes:** Push something one way and it pushes back the other way. everyday-physics/why-the-shower-curtain-moves-in (limits of Bernoulli) helps.

## Outline

1. **The equal-time story fails**: wind-tunnel smoke shows top air reaching the back edge first [verify], and the story cannot explain paper planes or flying upside down.
2. **A wing turns air down**: an airplane wing tilted into the airflow sends a stream of air downward behind it; pushing air down pushes the wing up.
3. **Pressure is the same story told at the surface**: turning the flow needs lower pressure above the wing and higher below; the pressure difference and the downward air are two views of one flow, not rival explanations.
4. **Tilt and stall**: lift grows with the wing's tilt up to a point; past it the airflow tears off the top surface and lift falls sharply, which is a stall.
5. **What this means for you**: hot air is thinner, so on hot days, and at high airfields, a plane needs more speed and a longer runway to take off [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Air over the top of a wing does not need to meet the air below it at the back edge, and in practice gets there first [verify].
- A wing makes lift by deflecting air downward.
- The low pressure above and the downward airflow describe the same lift, not two separate forces.
- Lift rises with the wing's tilt until the flow separates and the wing stalls, at around 15° for many wings [verify].
- Lift falls in proportion to air density, so heat and altitude both cut it [verify].
