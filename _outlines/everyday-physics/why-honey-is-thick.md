**Promise:** Explain viscosity as friction between layers of a flowing liquid, and why honey's thickness depends so steeply on water content and temperature.

**Trap:** "Honey is thick because it is heavy." Density and thickness are different properties: honey is only about 1.4 times as dense as water but thousands of times as viscous [verify], and dense mercury pours easily.

**Assumes:** Liquids are made of molecules that slide past each other. No module required.

## Outline

1. **Heavy is not thick**: density is mass per volume; viscosity is resistance to flowing; mercury and honey show they are independent.
2. **What viscosity is**: when one layer of liquid slides over the next, molecules carry motion between them and catch on each other; the harder that is, the thicker the liquid.
3. **Why sugar syrup is thick**: sugar molecules are large and form many hydrogen bonds with water and with each other, so they must break and remake many bonds to move past one another.
4. **Warmth and water both thin it**: a few degrees warmer or a few percent more water cuts honey's viscosity by large factors [verify], because each brings the sugar molecules nearer to sliding freely.
5. **What this means for you**: beekeepers and packers warm honey to pump and filter it, and the water content that sets its thickness also decides whether it will ferment [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Viscosity and density are separate properties; a liquid can be dense and runny or light and thick.
- Honey is roughly 1.4 times as dense as water and several thousand times as viscous at room temperature [verify].
- Honey's viscosity falls by about half for each few degrees of warming near room temperature [verify].
- Honey with more than about 18 to 20% water is runnier and at risk of fermenting [verify].
