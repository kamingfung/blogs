**Promise:** Explain how a fridge moves heat from its cold inside to the warm room, and why an open fridge door warms the kitchen.

**Trap:** "A fridge makes cold and pumps it into the box." There is no cold to make; a fridge pumps heat out of the box and dumps it, plus the motor's work, into the room.

**Assumes:** Heat flows from hot to cold on its own. everyday-physics/why-a-metal-spoon-feels-cold is useful background.

## Outline

1. **Heat only runs downhill on its own**: to move heat from a cold box to a warm room you must spend work, the way a pump spends work to lift water.
2. **Evaporation soaks up heat**: a liquid that boils takes in heat; the refrigerant boils at low pressure inside the box, below the box's temperature.
3. **Squeeze it and it gives the heat back**: the compressor raises the vapor's pressure and temperature, and the hot coils at the back condense it and shed heat to the room.
4. **The energy balance**: the room gets the heat from the box plus the electricity the compressor used, so an open fridge heats the kitchen; a home fridge moves about two to four units of heat per unit of electricity [verify].
5. **What this means for you**: packhouse cold rooms and forced-air coolers use the same cycle to pull field heat out of fruit quickly after harvest, which slows ripening and decay [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- A fridge moves heat; it does not create cold.
- The refrigerant takes in heat by boiling inside the box and gives it out by condensing in the coils outside.
- A fridge with its door open warms the room, because the heat it moves goes back into the same room along with the motor's heat.
- The colder the box and the warmer the room, the more work each unit of heat costs [verify].
