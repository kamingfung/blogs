**Promise:** Explain a battery as one chemical reaction split in two, with electrons sent through your device and ions through the battery, and say what sets its voltage.

**Trap:** "A battery is a tank of stored electricity that empties as electrons leave it." A battery stores chemical energy and holds the same number of electrons full or flat; it dies when the reacting chemicals are used up.

**Assumes:** Atoms can give up or take on electrons; a current is moving charge. No module required.

## Outline

1. **One reaction, two halves**: some chemicals want to swap electrons; keep them apart and the swap can only happen if electrons travel through an outside wire.
2. **Ions inside, electrons outside**: the material between the two ends lets ions through but not electrons, so the current in your device and the ions in the battery complete one loop.
3. **Voltage comes from the chemistry**: how strongly each side pulls on electrons sets the voltage, about 1.5 V for an alkaline cell and 3.6 to 3.7 V for a lithium-ion cell [verify]; size sets how long it lasts, not its voltage.
4. **Charging runs it backward, and cold slows it**: a rechargeable battery is one whose reaction can be pushed back by an outside supply; in the cold, ions move slowly and the battery delivers less.
5. **What this means for you**: field weather stations and soil sensors lose battery capacity in frost, so winter is when their batteries fail [verify].
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- A battery stores chemical energy, not electrons.
- The electrolyte carries ions but blocks electrons, which forces the electrons through the outside circuit.
- A battery's voltage is set by its two electrode chemistries, not by its size.
- A lithium-ion battery delivers noticeably less capacity below freezing than at room temperature [verify].
