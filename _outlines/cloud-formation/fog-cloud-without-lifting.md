**Promise:** Explain the three ways air near the ground reaches saturation without rising (cooling from below, moist air moving over a cold surface, and added vapor), and say which kind of fog to expect on a given night.

**Trap:** Fog is a cloud that sank to the ground; in fact it forms in place, because the air at the surface reached its dew point by cooling or moistening while staying where it is.

**Assumes:** cloud-formation/how-much-water-can-air-hold (saturation, relative humidity, dew point).

## Outline

1. **Same droplets, different route**: fog and a low cloud are the same thing; the only difference is how the air got saturated, and rising is just one route.
2. **Radiation fog**: clear, calm, humid nights; the ground cools the air from below until it hits the dew point; why it burns off from the edges and the top after sunrise.
3. **Advection fog**: moist air blown over cold water or cold ground; why coastal fog can last for days and needs wind, not calm.
4. **Adding water instead of removing heat**: steam fog over warm water and fog after rain on warm ground; a brief note that upslope fog is really lifting and belongs to the previous modules.
5. **What this means for you**: radiation fog and radiation frost need the same night; ground fog marks the cold spots in a field; persistent coastal fog and drizzle shape light and humidity for coastal farms; a dew-point forecast near the expected minimum temperature is the fog forecast.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Fog forms when air at the surface reaches its dew point without being lifted, by cooling from below, by moving over a colder surface, or by gaining vapor.
- Radiation fog needs a clear sky, light wind and moist air near the ground; strong wind usually prevents it by mixing in drier, warmer air from above.
- Advection fog can form in moderate wind and persist through the day, because its cause (the cold surface) does not go away at sunrise.
- When the forecast minimum temperature falls to the evening dew point, expect fog or dew; the dew point acts as a floor that slows the night's cooling.
