**Promise:** Explain why a curved water surface needs a higher vapor pressure to stay in balance than a flat one, and estimate for which drop sizes that matters.

**Trap:** At 100% relative humidity water stops evaporating; that holds only for a flat surface, while a small drop at 100% still evaporates, because its curved surface lets molecules escape more easily.

**Assumes:** cloud-formation/why-are-drops-round (surface tension and the Young-Laplace pressure); cloud-formation/how-much-water-can-air-hold (saturation vapor pressure).

## Outline

1. **Saturation was defined over a flat surface**: the saturation curve from earlier assumes a flat sheet of water; a drop is not flat.
2. **Curvature pushes molecules out**: the extra pressure inside a curved surface raises the chemical potential of the water, so a molecule at the surface of a small drop is held less tightly; the Kelvin equation as the result, in words first.
3. **How big is the effect**: the excess vapor pressure scales as one over the radius, negligible for a raindrop and large for a cluster of a few nanometers [verify].
4. **Big drops grow at the expense of small ones**: in a population of pure drops at one humidity, small ones evaporate and large ones grow; the same reason why a drop cannot start from nothing.
5. **What this means for you**: no direct field tie; for the student, it is the curvature term in every activation and nucleation scheme in a climate or air-quality model.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- The equilibrium vapor pressure over a curved water surface is higher than over a flat one, and the excess grows as the radius shrinks, roughly as one over the radius.
- For drops larger than about a micrometer the Kelvin excess is a fraction of a percent of relative humidity, while for drops of a few nanometers it is tens of percent or more [verify].
- At any fixed humidity above 100%, a pure water drop smaller than a critical radius evaporates and a larger one grows.
- The Kelvin effect alone explains why pure vapor needs a supersaturation the atmosphere never reaches to form drops on its own.
