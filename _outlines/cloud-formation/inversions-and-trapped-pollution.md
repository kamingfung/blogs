**Promise:** Name the three common ways an inversion forms (night cooling at the ground, sinking air aloft, cool marine air under warm air) and explain why each one caps how high smoke, dust and spray can mix.

**Trap:** Bad air on a still day means more was emitted; often the emissions are the same and the inversion has shrunk the volume of air they mix into.

**Assumes:** cloud-formation/lapse-rates-and-stability.

## Outline

1. **When temperature rises with height**: an inversion is the most stable layer there is; a parcel pushed up into it is instantly colder than its surroundings and sinks back.
2. **Three ways to make one**: radiation inversions after clear, calm nights; subsidence inversions under high pressure, where sinking air warms by compression; marine inversions where cool sea air sits under warm air from land.
3. **A lid on the mixing layer**: emissions spread through the depth below the lid, so the same emissions in a shallower layer give higher concentrations; multi-day stagnation episodes in valleys and basins.
4. **How a lid breaks**: daytime heating erodes a radiation inversion from below; wind and passing fronts remove the others; subsidence lids can last for days.
5. **What this means for you**: spraying under a surface inversion lets fine droplets drift sideways far from the target, which is why label guidance avoids calm, clear early mornings [verify]; stagnant high-pressure spells raise particulate readings; the warm air above a radiation inversion is what wind machines pull down on frost nights.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- In an inversion, temperature increases with height, so vertical mixing stops at its base or within it.
- For fixed emissions, a shallower mixing layer gives proportionally higher near-surface concentrations.
- Radiation inversions form on clear, calm nights and usually break by late morning, while subsidence inversions under persistent high pressure can last for several days [verify].
- Fine spray droplets released under a surface inversion stay concentrated near the ground and can drift laterally over long distances [verify].
