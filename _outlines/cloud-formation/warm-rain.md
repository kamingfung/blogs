**Promise:** Explain how a cloud that never freezes turns millions of tiny droplets into raindrops, and why condensation alone is too slow to do it.

**Trap:** Raindrops are cloud droplets that kept condensing until they got heavy; condensation slows as a drop grows, and rain in warm clouds needs a few larger drops to start sweeping up their neighbors.

**Assumes:** cloud-formation/why-cloud-droplets-dont-fall; cloud-formation/how-drops-merge.

## Outline

1. **The size gap**: a cloud droplet is roughly ten micrometers across and a raindrop roughly a millimeter, a million times more water [verify]; this module is about how that gap closes without ice.
2. **Condensation narrows, it does not widen**: the radius of a drop grows by condensation more slowly as it gets bigger, so a population of droplets becomes more uniform and growth stalls.
3. **Collection takes over**: a slightly larger drop falls faster, overtakes smaller ones and collects some of them; collision efficiency is low for very small collectors, so the process needs a head start [verify].
4. **Where the head start comes from**: giant sea-salt nuclei, turbulence, and time spent in the cloud; why clean marine clouds rain sooner than polluted continental ones.
5. **What this means for you**: tropical and coastal rain, and the drizzle under marine stratus, often form without ice, so they can fall from clouds whose tops are warmer than freezing [verify]; forecast models handle this step with a simple rate formula, a common source of drizzle and light-rain error.
6. **Key takeaways**

## Key takeaways

- Condensation alone cannot grow cloud droplets to raindrop size within a cloud's lifetime, because the radius grows more slowly as the drop gets bigger.
- Warm rain starts once a few drops exceed roughly 20 micrometers in radius, large enough to collect smaller droplets efficiently [verify].
- Clouds with fewer, larger droplets (typically clean marine air) start raining sooner than clouds with many small droplets.
- In the tropics, a large share of rain forms through warm-rain collection rather than ice processes [verify].
